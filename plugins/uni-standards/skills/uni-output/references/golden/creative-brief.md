## Why this brief works

Three things make it executable rather than decorative, and all three get skipped when a brief is rushed:

1. **A named Asset ID for every single deliverable**, before anything is designed. The media buyer maps files to placements without asking. A folder of `final_v2_REAL_final.png` costs an hour every launch.
2. **Hard rules stated once, at the top**, covering the things that would make the work legally or commercially wrong. Not style preferences - the things that get an ad rejected or annoy the retail partner.
3. **One message per job.** Written as a full sentence, not a theme. If it cannot be said in one sentence, it is two jobs.

## JOB 1 - Local showroom

**Campaign:** `Harlow | Google | BOF | Local-PMAX-[Town]`

**Landing page:** harlowshowroom.example

**Geo:** roughly 15-25 miles around the showroom

**Objective:** walk-ins and scheduled visits

**Audience:** nearby homeowners and renovators, plus designers and builders in the local design corridor

**One message:** See Harlow in person at [Town]. Pro-style ranges and full kitchen suites, walk-ins and appointments welcome.

**Must show or say:** the town, "Design Center", the street address, live displays, and a CTA of Schedule, Call or Visit. Soft retailer line optional.

**Do not:** national shipping or order-online as the primary CTA, misrepresent floor inventory, or collage all four product lines together.

**Copy seeds:** Visit Our NJ Showroom | [Town] Design Center | See Kitchen Suites Live | Schedule a Visit Today | For Designers & Builders

## Hard requirements

1. **Every deliverable has an Asset ID before design starts.** No exceptions, including the ones scheduled for later.
2. **One message per job, written as a full sentence.** If it takes two, it is two jobs.
3. **Hard rules stated once at the top**, covering rejection risk and channel-partner risk, not taste.
4. **A "must show" and an "avoid" for every product line.** The "avoid" prevents more revision rounds than the "must show."
5. **Competitor references are live ads**, linked from an ad transparency library, with screenshots. Never a mood board.
6. **Overlay lines come from `meta-ad-copy`**, so they are inside the limits, on-register and using cleared claims.
7. **Placeholder blocks stay in the document** until filled, so an unfinished asset is visible rather than forgotten.
8. **No pricing, no promotional percentages** unless the media buyer supplied live flight dates.
9. **Zero em dashes and en dashes.** Hyphens only.
10. **Notion spacing:** blank line before and after every heading, table and list.
11. **Internal only.** This document names the channel economics and the things we will not say. It never goes to the client.

## Variant: simple brief for a small batch

When the job is three display ads rather than 43 assets, the full structure is overkill. The short form keeps only what matters:

```
PROJECT DESCRIPTION   what is being promoted, one paragraph
OBJECTIVE             one number, one action
CUSTOMER AUDIENCE     who, where, what they care about
CURRENT ASSETS        links: landing page, testimonials, image files, brand guidelines
REFERENCES            2-3 competitor ads, linked

AD #1
  Main message / headline
  CTA
  Reference or mock            (link to a real image)
  Notes                        (the one specific direction that would otherwise be guessed)
  Dimensions                   (every size needed)

AD #2 ...
```

The short form still obeys every hard requirement above. It drops the size matrix, not the discipline.
