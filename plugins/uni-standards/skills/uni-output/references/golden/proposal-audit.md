## The architecture

Every UNI proposal follows the same five-beat problem-solving structure. Do not reorder it, and do not add beats.

| Beat | What it does | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Background & findings | States what we found in their account, with evidence | Earns the right to recommend anything |
| 2. Objectives & KPIs | Names the target in their language | Turns a complaint into a number |
| 3. Proposed solution | Numbered fixes, each mapped to a finding | Proves we are solving, not selling |
| 4. Timeline | Week-by-week, first two weeks specific | Makes it feel executable, not aspirational |
| 5. Success metrics | Metric, target, review date | Gives them a way to hold us to it |

**The rule that governs the whole document: every item in beat 3 must trace back to a specific finding in beat 1.** A recommendation with no finding behind it is a service being sold. A finding with no recommendation is a complaint. If a reader cannot draw the line, cut the item.

## Background & Challenges

Voyara runs travel-based language courses and volunteer programs, including a medical placement track for recent graduates.

- Past Meta campaigns drove volume but not quality. The language program generated leads that did not qualify; volunteer campaigns delivered clicks under $1 with zero conversions.
- **Google Ads has no conversion tracking installed.** Recent campaigns ran with no attributed conversions at all, so nothing in the account has ever been optimised against a real outcome.
- Campaign structure is broad and undifferentiated. One example: Italian-language audiences receiving English-language creative for a program that is not offered in their market.

## Objectives & KPIs

- **Primary:** generate qualified leads for the volunteer and medical programs through Meta Ads
- **Secondary:** install conversion tracking on Google Ads so lead attribution and optimisation become possible
- **Goal:** 15 or more qualified leads per month within 60 days

---

## Proposed Solution

1. **Conversion tracking fix (Meta + Google)**

   Installs the measurement layer the account has been missing, so cost-per-lead optimisation becomes possible at all. *Addresses finding 2.*

2. **Volunteer & medical program funnel**

   A dedicated funnel built around what the audience is actually buying, which is travel plus clinical hours, not a course. Custom landing page recommended. *Addresses finding 1.*

3. **Creative & copy revamp**

   Purpose-led angles tested against feature-led ones. Both lengths run as separate ads. *Addresses finding 1.*

4. **Audience restructuring**

   Segmented by program intent (medical, gap year, social impact) and by geography, starting with the markets the programs are actually offered in. *Addresses finding 3.*

5. **Performance reporting**

   Bi-weekly performance notes and a monthly review covering cost per lead, conversion rate and top creatives.

## Hard requirements

Non-negotiable for every proposal or audit built from this sample.

1. **No pricing anywhere.** No fees, retainers, hourly rates, package tiers, or scope-to-fee mapping. If the prospect needs numbers, that is Sean's document, not this one.
2. **Every finding carries a screenshot** from the client's own account.
3. **Every proposed item names the finding it addresses.** Delete anything that cannot.
4. **Length ceiling: one screen per beat.** If Background runs past four bullets, the extra findings belong in an appendix or in the call.
5. **Targets come from the client's own data**, never from a category benchmark, and every figure carries its date range and source.
6. **No superlatives and no guarantees.** "We will get you 15 leads" is a promise we cannot keep. "Goal: 15 or more leads per month within 60 days" is a target we can be held to.
7. **Zero em dashes and en dashes.** Hyphens only.
8. **Notion spacing:** blank line before and after every heading, table and list.
9. **One CTA at the end.** A call link and a reply option. Not three paths.
